CASE ID: PHISH-001
CASE TYPE: Suspected Phishing Email
STATUS: Under Investigation

========================================
1. INITIAL ALERT
========================================

An employee reported a suspicious email claiming to be from Microsoft Security.

The message stated that the user's Microsoft 365 password would expire and that the account would be suspended unless immediate verification was completed.

========================================
2. INITIAL OBSERVATIONS
========================================

Finding 1 - Suspicious Sender Domain

Displayed Sender:
Microsoft Security

Sender Address:
security@microsoft-support-verification.com

Observation:
The message claims to originate from Microsoft Security, but the sender domain does not match the expected official Microsoft domain.

Assessment:
Potential brand impersonation.

----------------------------------------

Finding 2 - Reply-To Mismatch

Reply-To:
accountverify@outlook-security-help.com

Observation:
The Reply-To domain differs from the sender domain.

Assessment:
This inconsistency increases suspicion and requires further investigation.

----------------------------------------

Finding 3 - Urgency / Social Engineering

Subject:
URGENT: Your Microsoft 365 Password Expires Today

Message:
The recipient is warned that the account will be suspended unless immediate action is taken.

Assessment:
The message uses urgency and fear to pressure the recipient into acting quickly.

----------------------------------------

Finding 4 - Suspicious Verification URL

URL:
hxxps://microsoft-login-verification[.]com/verify

Observation:
The URL uses Microsoft-related keywords but does not use the official Microsoft domain.

Assessment:
Potential credential-phishing URL.

========================================
3. EMAIL AUTHENTICATION
========================================

SPF: PASS
DKIM: PASS
DMARC: PASS

Assessment:

Passing SPF, DKIM and DMARC does not prove that the email is legitimate.

The results indicate that the message was authenticated for the sending domain, but they do not prove that the domain belongs to Microsoft.

========================================
4. EXTRACTED IoCs

========================================
6. ANALYST VERDICT
========================================

Verdict: SUSPICIOUS

The email contains multiple phishing indicators, including Microsoft
brand impersonation, a non-official sender domain, a mismatched
Reply-To domain, urgency-based social engineering, and a suspicious
account-verification URL.

Although SPF, DKIM and DMARC pass, these results authenticate the
sending domain and do not establish that Microsoft sent the message.

Because PHISH-001 uses fictional training infrastructure and lacks
independent threat-intelligence confirmation, the email is classified
as suspicious rather than conclusively malicious.

========================================
7. EVIDENCE CORRELATION
========================================

Email Content:
The message impersonates Microsoft Security and pressures the recipient
to verify an account immediately to avoid suspension.

Sender Identity:
The sender uses a Microsoft-themed domain that does not match the
expected official Microsoft domain.

Reply-To:
The Reply-To address uses a different domain from the sender,
increasing suspicion.

URL:
The verification link uses a Microsoft-themed domain rather than the
expected official Microsoft domain.

Email Authentication:
SPF, DKIM and DMARC are shown as PASS in the training sample.
These results authenticate the sending domain but do not establish
that the sender is actually Microsoft.

IoCs:
Sender domain, Reply-To domain, URL domain and full URL were extracted
for investigation.

Threat Intelligence:
VirusTotal, urlscan and AbuseIPDB were studied using separate safe
baseline/test indicators to understand reputation, domain
infrastructure, relationships and IP abuse reporting.

The fictional PHISH-001 domains were not treated as real malicious
infrastructure, and no threat-intelligence results were fabricated
for them.

Overall Correlation:
Multiple independent characteristics of the email are consistent with
a credential-phishing scenario: brand impersonation, urgency,
sender/Reply-To inconsistency and a suspicious account-verification
URL.

A final analyst verdict will be assigned after considering the
combined evidence and the limitations of the training dataset.

========================================
8. RESPONSE, CONTAINMENT & ESCALATION
========================================

Initial User Exposure:
The reporting user confirmed that the suspicious email was opened,
but the embedded URL was not clicked.

No attachment was downloaded, no reply was sent, and no credentials
were submitted.

Assessment:
No evidence of credential exposure or account compromise was identified
for the reporting user.

----------------------------------------
Campaign Scope
----------------------------------------

Total matching messages identified: 12

Successfully delivered: 9
Quarantined: 3
Known link clicks: 0

Assessment:
The activity affected multiple mailboxes and is therefore treated as
a broader phishing campaign rather than an isolated email.

----------------------------------------
Recommended Containment
----------------------------------------

1. Remove or quarantine the 9 delivered messages where supported.

2. Notify affected users not to click links, download attachments,
   reply to the sender, or provide credentials.

3. Block confirmed malicious/suspicious indicators according to
   organizational security procedures.

4. Search the email environment for additional messages containing
   matching sender addresses, domains, URLs, or subjects.

5. Continue monitoring for additional campaign activity.

----------------------------------------
Escalation Criteria
----------------------------------------

Escalation to L2 / Incident Response is recommended if evidence shows
that a user:

- Clicked the phishing URL.
- Submitted credentials.
- Downloaded or executed a suspicious attachment.
- Shows suspicious authentication activity.
- Shows evidence of unauthorized account activity.

----------------------------------------
Credential Exposure Response
----------------------------------------

If credentials were submitted to the suspected phishing site, the
incident should be treated as a potential credential compromise.

Recommended actions, subject to organizational authorization and
incident-response procedures:

- Escalate immediately to L2 / Incident Response.
- Initiate or request a password reset.
- Revoke active sessions/tokens where appropriate.
- Review MFA activity and configuration.
- Investigate recent sign-in history.
- Review suspicious source IP addresses, locations and devices.
- Check for unauthorized mailbox rules or account changes.
- Continue campaign-wide containment and monitoring.

Note:
An L1 analyst should only perform containment actions that are
authorized by the organization's procedures and access permissions.
